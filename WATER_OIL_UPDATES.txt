================================================================================
PHYSICS CHANGES
================================================================================

Surface Tension:
   Before: 0.07 N/m (water-air interface)
   After: 0.025 N/m (water-silicone oil interface)
   Impact: Lower surface tension means easier droplet deformation

Density Ratio:
   Before: 1000:1 (water to air)
   After: 997:960 ≈ 1.04:1 (water to oil)
   Impact: More comparable densities, different settling behavior

Viscosity Ratio:
   Before: 1e-3:1.8e-5 ≈ 55:1 (water to air)
   After: 1e-3:96e-3 ≈ 1:96 (water is less viscous than oil)
   Impact: Water droplets will move faster through viscous oil, shear layer more important

Reynolds Number (approx):
   Re = rho * u * D / mu
   Before: 1000 * 0.3 * 0.004 / 1e-3 ≈ 1200 (laminar but higher)
   After: 960 * 0.3 * 0.004 / (960*1e-4) ≈ 12 (much more laminar)
   Impact: More creeping flow behavior, droplets move more slowly relative to continuous phase

Capillary Number (approx):
   Ca = mu * u / sigma
   Before: 1e-3 * 0.3 / 0.07 ≈ 0.004
   After: 96e-3 * 0.3 / 0.025 ≈ 1.15 (much higher!)
   Impact: Significant droplet deformation expected, interfacial effects less dominant

================================================================================
EXPECTED BEHAVIOR CHANGES
================================================================================

1. Flow Characteristics:
   - Much lower Reynolds numbers (Re ~12 vs ~1200)
   - More Stokes-like flow, creeping motion
   - Droplets will appear to move more slowly
   - Less mixing due to viscous dissipation

2. Droplet Dynamics:
   - Lower capillary number (higher in updated system) increases deformation
   - Water droplets may deform more under flow
   - Reduced buoyancy differences (similar densities)
   - Viscous drag dominates over inertial effects

3. Interfacial Tension Effects:
   - Lower surface tension (0.025 vs 0.07 N/m)
   - Droplets less "round", more elongated
   - Easier to break up in shear flow
   - Better for microfluidic applications

4. Simulation Performance:
   - Smaller Courant numbers needed due to lower velocity scales
   - More iterations may be needed for convergence
   - Smaller time steps recommended
   - Simulation may run slightly longer relative to physical time
